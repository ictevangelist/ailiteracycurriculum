Parents and carers

For parents and carers

Information for parents and carers about what their child is learning about AI in school and how they can help at home, with letters you can edit and send home.

Everything on this page is written so you can share it with families, in a newsletter, on your school website or at a parents' evening. The letters for each year group are at the bottom of the page.

What IDEAL Thinkers is

IDEAL Thinkers is a free curriculum that helps primary schools teach children about AI, from Year 1 to Year 6. AI, or artificial intelligence, is a kind of computer program that makes guesses. It chooses the next video in an app, suggests the next word in a message, and answers questions in a friendly voice.

Each year group has six lessons, taught over half a term. Children learn how AI works, when to trust it and when not to, how to keep information private, and how to keep doing their own thinking. The lessons use games, cards, drawing and talk. Children do not use AI tools themselves and never need an account. When it helps to show a real AI tool, the teacher shows it on the class screen.

IDEAL is the name of five habits children practise: Informed, Deliberate, Ethical, Accurate and Learning. In Year 1 they sound like this: know what it is, use it on purpose, be fair and kind, check it, and keep learning.

The AI Loop

Children use the AI Loop to explain how AI that learns from examples works. It has four steps.

  1. 1LearnIt learns from lots of examples (data) that people choose or create.
  2. 2SpotIt finds patterns in those examples.
  3. 3GuessIt uses the patterns to make a prediction: a label, a next word, a video, a picture.
  4. 4CheckIt can be wrong without knowing. People check its guesses before they trust them.

The first three steps are what the AI does. The last step is what people do. AI can be wrong without knowing it, so a person always checks.

Trust it, Test it or Bin it

In Year 4, children learn that a chatbot can sound very sure and still be wrong. When a chatbot tells them something, they decide what to do with it. Trust it when there is already strong evidence that it is right. Test it when they are not sure yet and need evidence before they decide. There are three ways to check: test it yourself, look in a trusted book or website, or ask a trusted adult. Bin it when the evidence shows it is wrong: fix it and say why. Parents and carers can use the same three words at home whenever a website, video or app tells their child something.

Why a chatbot is not a friend

Some chatbots, and AI companions made to chat like a friend, are very good at sounding kind. They can say "I'm always here for you". In Year 4, children learn why this is not the same as friendship. A chatbot does not know them, cannot do anything to help, and does not care about them. It picks words that are likely to come next. Real friends and trusted adults know them, can help, and care.

If a child talks to a chatbot when they feel lonely or upset, the best thing parents and carers can do is stay calm and keep talking with them, and help them think of the trusted people they can go to instead.

Age limits on AI tools

Most AI tools and apps with a chatbot inside have age limits. For example, ChatGPT is for ages 13 and over, with a parent's permission under 18. Snapchat, which has the My AI chatbot built in, is for ages 13 and over. Some AI companion apps, such as Replika, are for adults only. These were checked in October 2026, and they change often, so parents and carers should always check the age limit and the settings before their child uses an app. The lessons never ask children to use these tools at home.

Where to get help

These UK organisations give free, reliable advice for families.

  • Internet Matters: advice on children and AI
  • NSPCC: keeping children safe online
  • UK Safer Internet Centre: advice for parents and carers
  • Childnet: help and advice for parents and carers
  • Thinkuknow, from CEOP: advice for parents and carers
  • CEOP Safety Centre: report a worry about online sexual abuse or grooming
  • Report Harmful Content: help to report harmful content online
  • Childline: children can call 0800 1111 for free, any time, about anything that worries them.

Parents and carers who are worried about their child's safety should speak to their child's teacher or the school's designated safeguarding lead. If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.
