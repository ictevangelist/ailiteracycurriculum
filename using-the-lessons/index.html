The Curriculum Guide (a one document overview)

Using IDEAL Thinkers in your school? Tell me you're using it, and ask for support with implementation if you'd like it.

Starting out

  1. Where it fits. Teach each year's six lessons in order, as one half term of computing. Lessons with RSHE links, such as Year 4 Lesson 2, can be taught in RSHE time, but keep them in sequence.
  2. Starting above Year 1. In the first year, pupils in Years 2 to 6 have not met the earlier units. Before Lesson 1, introduce Bleep, the IDEAL habits and the IDEAL moment routine (using your key stage's wording) and the AI Loop, then the ideas that year's unit builds on. How much that takes depends on the year group: each year page lists the slides to use and roughly how long to allow, under "Starting Year N with pupils new to IDEAL Thinkers" (Year 2, Year 3, Year 4, Year 5, Year 6). Treat words marked "Already taught" as new. A short introduction gives pupils the words and routines, not the years of practice behind them, so expect to revisit these ideas.
  3. What to assume. Assume no AI knowledge beyond the earlier units. Children's experience at home varies widely; no lesson depends on it, and you never need to ask which apps they use.
  4. Preparation. Read the six plans at the start of the half term. First time through, allow up to twice the stated preparation time for printing and cutting, and keep each lesson's cards in an envelope for next year. Year 5 Lesson 6 needs clean recycling collected in advance, and Year 6 Lesson 6 needs a small panel invited.
  5. Subject knowledge. Each plan's Teacher subject knowledge section is all you need; read it the day before. Practise two things beforehand: building the Scratch program (Year 5 Lesson 1) and running Teachable Machine (Year 5 Lesson 6).
  6. New to AI tools? 32 of the 36 lessons use no AI tool at all. The four demonstration lessons give the exact steps and words to type, and each can be taught with no tool.
  7. Devices and school policy. Every lesson needs only a screen for the slides. If your school has not approved a suitable tool, use the prepared results or the unplugged version. Never use a personal account.
  8. Misconceptions. Each plan lists the likely ones. If an exit ticket shows one is common, reteach it briefly at the start of the next lesson and add a Remember When question on it.
  9. If a lesson runs long. Stop at a natural break, do the exit ticket, and finish the rest at the start of the next lesson in place of Link It. For 45 minute lessons, use the 45 minute version in the plan.
  10. Checking it works. A subject leader needs only what the lessons already produce. Once per unit, look at each class tracker's summary and three pupils' IDEAL Challenge work (one secure, one developing, one not yet), and ask the teacher two questions: which lesson ran over, and which misconception came up most.
  11. Celebrating success. Give each pupil an IDEAL Thinkers passport for the year, initial a circle after each lesson, and award the certificate when all six are full: passport and certificate.

Teacher CPD

Each unit has a 20 minute staff meeting deck with presenter notes: the big question, the core idea in plain terms, the AI Loop and any model the unit uses, the most common misconceptions, safeguarding and DSL briefing points, a signature activity for staff to try, and what the IDEAL Challenge assessment looks for.
