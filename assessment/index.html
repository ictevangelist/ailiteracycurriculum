Assessment

Assessment

Each lesson has three "I can" statements and a three question exit ticket. Each IDEAL Challenge doubles as the end of unit assessment, where pupils show what they understand on their own.

Where the evidence comes from

The exit ticket is the last slide of every lesson, and its questions and answers are in the lesson plan. One question always asks pupils to apply the idea to something they have not seen in the lesson. Sort the answers into got it, nearly and not yet: the middle pile chooses the next Remember When question, and you reteach briefly if the last pile is large.

At the end of each unit, the IDEAL Challenge and that unit's exit tickets give one judgement per pupil for each of the five progression strands. Team work is useful feedback, but every judgement rests on what each pupil shows on their own.

When a pupil has completed all six lessons, you can celebrate it with the IDEAL Thinkers passport and certificate: a circle initialled after each lesson, and a certificate when the unit is done.

Secure, developing and not yet

Secure means the pupil explains the idea with reasons, without prompting, and applies it to a new case. Developing means they show the idea with prompting, or explain part of it. Not yet means the idea is not there yet, often with a common misconception. The tracker gives what secure looks like in each key stage, for each strand.

Seeing progress across the years

Keep one tracker file per class from Year 1 to Year 6. Each September, duplicate the tracker tab and choose the new year group, so earlier years stay visible. A subject leader needs only the class summary and three pupils' IDEAL Challenge work once per unit.
