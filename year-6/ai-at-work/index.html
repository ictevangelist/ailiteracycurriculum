Year 6, Lesson 3

AI at work

AI is changing jobs, and some things stay human.

IDEAL: Informed, LearningILLinks: DL, careers

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 3 of 6 in Year 6: AI, Society and the Future
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain what automation is.
  • I can sort the tasks in real jobs into AI could help, AI could do and needs a person.
  • I can explain which human skills stay important, including in my own learning.
You need
Printed task cards in job envelopes (slides 16 and 17), sorting mats (slide 18)
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slides 16 and 17 (task cards), one set per team of four; cut them up and put each job's four cards in an envelope labelled Vet, Farmer, Teacher or Chef. Print slide 18 (sorting mat), one per team, ideally at A3, or label three areas of each table. No devices are needed.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
None
Prior learning
Builds on learning from earlier years. Before teaching this lesson

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • Rule based programs follow rules people wrote; machine learning learns from examples.
  • AI guesses can be wrong, so a person checks the important ones.
  • If AI does the thinking, you do not do the learning.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 5 Lesson 1: Rules or examples? (slides 5, 6 and 13)
  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 3: The Mystery Rule (slide 8)
  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 5: AI Helpers Hall of Fame (slides 4 and 8)
  • Earlier year Year 5 Lesson 5: Climb the STAIR (slide 6)
  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 3: Try it first (slide 12)
  • Earlier year Year 3 Lesson 4: Hint, not answer (slide 6)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 2 Lesson 5, Year 5 Lesson 1, Year 6 Lesson 1, Year 6 Lesson 2. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 3 and 4 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Introduce or retrieve: show Year 5 Lesson 1 slide 6, two ways to build a sorter (3 minutes). Retrieve: show Year 2 Lesson 5 slide 8, what it guesses and who checks (2 minutes). Introduce or retrieve: show Year 5 Lesson 5 slide 6, Plan, use, prove (2 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils sort real job tasks and see that care, judgement and responsibility stay human, and learning needs their own thinking.

The signature activity

Job Swap Shop: pupils sort the tasks in real jobs into "AI could help", "AI could do" and "needs a person", then invent a job of the future.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What is automation? 2. Give one task AI could help with, and one task that needs a person. Say why. 3. Why should a pupil not let AI write their story for them?

Look for pupils who ask what happens if the AI is wrong and who should answer for it, and who can explain why learning tasks need their own thinking even when AI could do them.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Staying in charge; Learning Well, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
