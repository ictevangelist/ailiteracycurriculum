Year 6, Lesson 1

Real or generated?

AI can fake images, voices and video, so we need a checking routine and firm rules about real people.

IDEAL: Accurate, EthicalAELinks: DC, DL

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 1 of 6 in Year 6: AI, Society and the Future
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain what generated pictures, voices and videos are, and why they fool us.
  • I can use Pause, Look, Ask, Check before I believe or share something.
  • I can explain why we never make or share AI pictures of real people.
You need
Printed exhibit cards, sealed check card envelopes, case files (slides 19 to 23), A4 booklets
Preparation
About 35 minutes the first time, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Read the safeguarding row above. Print the exhibit cards (slides 19 and 20) once, at A3 if possible, and put them around the room as a gallery. Print the check cards (slides 21 and 22), one set per team of four; fold each card and seal it in an envelope numbered 1 to 6, so each team carries its own six envelopes. Print one case file (slide 23) per team. Fold some A4 sheets into small booklets for Own It. No devices are needed. If you choose to show the flamingo photo or the 2023 prize winner from the news reports in the sources, preview them first; never display the deepfake video or any faked image of a real person.

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • AI makes its output from patterns learned in examples, and cannot check it is true.
  • Check a claim against evidence or a trusted source, not how sure it seems.
  • Consent is a real yes, freely given by someone who understands.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, the AI Loop, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 3: The Mystery Rule (slide 8)
  • Earlier year Year 4 Lesson 1: What comes next? (slide 8)
  • Earlier year Year 4 Lesson 3: Sure isn't right (slides 5 and 10)
  • Earlier year Year 5 Lesson 3: Whose is it anyway? (slide 5)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 5, Year 4 Lesson 3, Year 5 Lesson 3, Year 5 Lesson 6. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 3 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve or introduce: show Year 1 Lesson 3 slide 8, then this lesson's slide 6 (3 minutes). Introduce: show Year 4 Lesson 3 slide 10 and say a trusted source settles it, not a feeling (3 minutes). Introduce: show Year 5 Lesson 3 slide 5 for consent (2 minutes). Remember When question 1 needs Year 5 Lesson 6: if not taught, show Year 5 Lesson 6 slides 9 to 11 as an example (3 minutes) or skip it.

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn a checking routine for pictures, voices and videos and firm rules about real people.

The signature activity

The Gallery of Fakes: pupils hunt for clues, build the class Pause, Look, Ask, Check routine and agree the rule: we never make or share AI pictures, voices or videos of real people.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What does generated mean? 2. A video shows a famous footballer saying school is shut tomorrow. What do you do at Pause, Look, Ask and Check? 3. Give one reason why we never make an AI picture of a real person.

Look for pupils who reach a verdict from what a trusted source says rather than from a clue alone, and who talk about the person in the picture as well as whether the picture is clever.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Safe, fair and honest, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
