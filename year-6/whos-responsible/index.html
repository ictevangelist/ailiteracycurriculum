Year 6, Lesson 5

Who's responsible?

When AI makes a decision, people must be able to explain it and answer for it.

IDEAL: Ethical, InformedEILinks: DC, English

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 5 of 6 in Year 6: AI, Society and the Future
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain what accountable and transparent mean.
  • I can use evidence from a real case to explain who should answer for a mistake.
  • I can decide who is responsible and write recommendations.
You need
Printed witness cards, investigation sheets and responsibility scales (slides 16 to 19), a tennis ball
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slides 16 and 17 (witness cards), one set of four per team, and cut them up. Print slide 18 (investigation sheet) and slide 19 (responsibility scale), one of each per team, ideally the scale at A3. A tennis ball or racket makes a good prop. No devices are needed; if you show news coverage of the incident, preview it first.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
None
Prior learning

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • People make AI and automatic systems, and people must stay in charge of them.
  • Some decisions need a person who can answer for them.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 6: Boss of the Bot (slides 9 and 10)
  • This unit Year 6 Lesson 3: AI at work (slides 10 and 18)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 6, Year 5 Lesson 2, Year 6 Lesson 3, Year 6 Lesson 4. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 4 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve or introduce: show Year 1 Lesson 6 slide 10, Robot Rules (2 minutes). Retrieve or introduce: show Year 6 Lesson 3 slide 18, the sorting mat tests (2 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils use a real case to show that people, not systems, are accountable, and that responsibility can be shared.

The signature activity

The Investigation: when an automatic line calling system at a tennis tournament fails, pupils interview the people involved, decide who is responsible and write recommendations.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What does accountable mean? 2. What does transparent mean? 3. In the Wimbledon case, why did people, not the system, have to answer for the mistake?

Look for pupils who base verdicts on evidence from the cards, who see that responsibility can be shared, and who distinguish accountable (answering for it and fixing it) from transparent (being open about how it works).

This evidence feeds the tracker for Staying in charge; Safe, fair and honest, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
