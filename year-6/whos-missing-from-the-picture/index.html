Who's missing from the picture?

AI can repeat and magnify the patterns, and the unfairness, in its data.

IDEAL: Ethical, AccurateEALinks: DC

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 2 of 6 in Year 6: AI, Society and the Future
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can use data to compare pictures of people.
  • I can explain how AI can repeat and magnify unfair patterns.
  • I can suggest fair ways to fix the recipe.
You need
Sticky notes and pencils for everyone, printed tally and recipe sheets (slides 18 and 19)
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Give every pupil a sticky note and a pencil, without saying what they will draw. Print slide 18 (tally sheet), one per team of four, and slide 19 (recipe sheet), one per pair. Clear a space on the board for the class tally. Do not hint at the drawing before slide 5, and do not use the word stereotype until slide 7. No devices or AI tools are needed: the AI results come from a 2025 pilot study shared online before peer review, on slide 10.

None needed

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • Bias is AI working better for some people again and again, often from unbalanced data.
  • AI learns its patterns from training data, so it repeats what is common there.
  • A specific prompt leaves the AI less to fill in than a vague one.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, the AI Loop, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 5 Lesson 4: The Welcome Bot (slides 5, 9 and 10)
  • Earlier year Year 5 Lesson 2: Train the sorter (slides 5 and 11)
  • Earlier year Year 4 Lesson 4: Ask it better (slides 6 and 8)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 2 Lesson 2, Year 5 Lesson 2, Year 5 Lesson 4, Year 6 Lesson 1. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 3 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Introduce: show Year 5 Lesson 4 slides 5 and 10 and say bias means the same people let down again and again (4 minutes). Introduce: show Year 5 Lesson 2 slide 5 for training data (2 minutes). Retrieve or introduce: show Year 4 Lesson 4 slide 8, vague versus specific (2 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils use data to show that AI image tools can repeat and magnify stereotypes, and how fixes must be fair and accurate.

The signature activity

Draw, Tally, Compare: pupils quickly draw "a scientist", tally their own results, compare them with what image generators tend to produce, then fix the recipe.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What is a stereotype? 2. Why do AI image tools often show scientists as men in lab coats? 3. Name one way to fix the recipe.

Look for pupils who use numbers from the tally as evidence, who link the AI's pattern to its training data, and who check a fix for accuracy as well as fairness.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Safe, fair and honest, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
