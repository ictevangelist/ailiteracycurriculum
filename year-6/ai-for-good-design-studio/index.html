Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 6 of 6 in Year 6: AI, Society and the Future
Time
Two sessions of 60 minutes
Pupils learn
  • I can choose a real problem and decide whether AI is the right tool.
  • I can design an AI helper on the AI Loop and test it with an IDEAL audit.
  • I can explain how I will use AI for learning and prove the learning is mine.
You need
Printed cards and sheets (slides 19 to 22), A3 paper, real problems list, a timer
Preparation
About 15 minutes before Session 1 and 10 minutes before Session 2, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge. Plan ahead: Two sessions; brief the panel before Session 2.
What to prepare

Before Session 1: print slide 19 (design question cards), one set per team of four, and give each team a sheet of A3 paper. Make a short list of real problems in your school or community, or use these: lost property piling up; litter on the way to school; recycling put in the wrong bins; long queues at lunch; library books hard to find; plants in the school garden dying in the holidays; new pupils who do not know anyone yet; younger pupils who need more reading practice. Avoid problems about named people. Before Session 2: print slide 20 (IDEAL audit cards), one set per team; slide 21 (panel score sheet), one per panel member; and slide 22 (AI learning promise cards), one per pupil. Brief the panel using the panel briefing below. Set up a visible timer for the pitches. No pupil devices or accounts are needed.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Teach Lessons 1 to 5 first. This lesson assesses them, so it is not a starting point for the unit.

What this lesson adds

Pupils apply the whole unit independently, which gives the evidence for the tracker.

The signature activity

Teams scope a real problem in school or the community, design an AI helper using the AI Loop, run an IDEAL audit, pitch to a panel, and each pupil writes an AI learning promise for secondary school.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. A school wants an AI camera to spot who drops litter. Give one way it could fall short, and who must check. 2. Explain one change your team made after the IDEAL audit, and why. 3. Why is it good news when an IDEAL audit finds a problem?

Look for pupils who question whether AI is needed at all, who name whose data a design uses and who could be left out, who say who checks and who is accountable without prompting, and whose promises are specific and personal.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Staying in charge; Safe, fair and honest; Learning Well; Design and create, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
