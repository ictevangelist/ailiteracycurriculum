Year 2, Lesson 4

Keep it to yourself

Some things we never tell a machine.

IDEAL: EthicalELinks: DC

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 4 of 6 in Year 2: Using AI Wisely
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can say what personal information is.
  • I can sort questions into Safe to Say and Keep to Myself.
  • I can say what to do if a machine asks for something private.
You need
Printed room signs (slide 16) and question cards (slide 17), Bleep, paper, colouring pencils
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slide 16 (room signs), on A3 if you can, and stick Safe to Say and Keep to Myself on opposite sides of the room with a clear path between. Print slide 17 (Bleep's question cards): one set for you to read from and one set per table for pupils who sort at their seats. Have your Bleep ready. Plain paper and colouring pencils for Own It. Read the safeguarding row above and know who your Designated Safeguarding Lead is today.

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

No formal prerequisites: this lesson starts from pupils' everyday experience.

Routines it uses: Bleep, IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

Nothing earlier is needed.

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 6, Year 2 Lesson 1, Year 2 Lesson 2, Year 2 Lesson 3. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Nothing to do: start the lesson as planned.

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn what personal information is and the routine Stop, Keep it, Tell when a machine asks for it.

The signature activity

Bleep Wants to Know: Bleep asks friendly questions and pupils sort them into Safe to Say and Keep to Myself, then practise what to do.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. Bleep asks where you live. Safe to Say or Keep to Myself? 2. A game asks "What is your school called?" Safe to Say or Keep to Myself? How do you know? 3. A game asks for your password. What do you do?

Look for pupils who use the test (does it tell someone who I am or where to find me?) rather than guessing, who say the routine without prompting, and who can name a grown-up to tell.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Safe, fair and honest; Staying in charge, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
