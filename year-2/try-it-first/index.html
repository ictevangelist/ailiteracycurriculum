Year 2, Lesson 3

Try it first

My brain grows when I do the hard thinking.

IDEAL: Learning, DeliberateLDLinks: DL

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 3 of 6 in Year 2: Using AI Wisely
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can say why my brain grows when I do the hard thinking.
  • I can try it first before I get help.
  • I can tell a helper from a doer.
You need
Printed Helper or Doer card sets (slide 17), card bookmarks (slide 18), whiteboards, Bleep
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slide 17 (Helper or Doer? cards), one set per pair, and cut them out; give each pair a sheet folded in half and labelled Helper and Doer. Print slide 18 (bookmarks) on card, one per pupil. Have your Bleep ready. Whiteboards and pens for the puzzle rounds, colouring pencils for Own It. Draw a two column tally labelled Round 1 and Round 2 on the board for the Brain check.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
None
Prior learning
No formal prerequisites.

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

No formal prerequisites: this lesson starts from pupils' everyday experience.

Routines it uses: Bleep, IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

Nothing earlier is needed.

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 5, Year 1 Lesson 6, Year 2 Lesson 1, Year 2 Lesson 2. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Nothing to do: start the lesson as planned.

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn that doing the hard thinking themselves makes learning stick, and to use help on purpose after trying.

The signature activity

Helper or Doer? Bleep keeps offering to finish pupils' puzzles; the class discovers what they lose when Bleep does it, and when Bleep's help is useful.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. Why should I try it first? 2. Bleep gives you a clue. Helper or doer? 3. You are stuck on a word in your book. Bleep reads the whole page for you. Helper or doer? What would a helper do?

Look for pupils who explain why trying first matters (rather than only that it is a rule), who can say when help is useful, and who try before asking during the rest of the day.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Learning Well; Staying in charge, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
