Year 2, Lesson 2

Missing examples

When examples are missing, AI gets things wrong for some people and things.

IDEAL: EthicalELinks: CS

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 2 of 6 in Year 2: Using AI Wisely
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can say what happens when examples are missing.
  • I can find the missing examples in a pack.
  • I can explain why AI should work for everyone.
You need
Printed bird card packs in envelopes (slide 17), rule strips (slide 18), Bleep
Preparation
About 15 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slide 17 and cut out the bird cards. Make one pack per group of four or five: about two groups each with Pack A (blue, small birds that fly), Pack B (orange, birds of different sizes that fly) and Pack C (green, all sorts, including a bird that cannot fly). Keep each pack in a labelled envelope and do not tell groups the packs are different. Print slide 18: the test birds are for you to hold up, and each group needs one Bleep's rule strip. Have your Bleep ready. Plain paper and colouring pencils for Own It.

Pupil devices
None needed

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • AI learns from examples, and lots of different examples give better guesses.
  • AI spots what its examples share and uses that pattern as a rule for new things.

Routines it uses: Bleep, IDEAL moments, the AI Loop, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 2: Teaching Bleep (slides 7 to 11)
  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 3: The Mystery Rule (slides 6 to 8)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 1, Year 1 Lesson 2, Year 1 Lesson 3, Year 2 Lesson 1. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 3 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Introduce: Year 1 Lesson 2 slides 7, 8 and 11 (6 minutes) and Year 1 Lesson 3 slides 6 and 7 (5 minutes); otherwise retrieve slide 2.

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn that missing examples make AI wrong for some things or people, which is unfair, and people fix it.

The signature activity

Build a Bird: groups teach a partner what a bird is from a card pack, then test with a penguin, an ostrich and a kiwi to see whose pack let them down.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What goes wrong if Bleep's examples are missing some birds? 2. Bleep learned "dog" only from big dogs. A tiny dog comes in. What goes wrong, and how do we fix it? 3. How can we make Bleep fair?

Look for pupils who explain a wrong answer by pointing to what was missing from the examples, rather than blaming Bleep, and who link that to fairness.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Safe, fair and honest, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
