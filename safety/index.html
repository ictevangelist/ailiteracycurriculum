Safety

Technology, safety and safeguarding

Every lesson can be taught without pupil devices. Concepts outlast tools.

Unplugged first

Every lesson can be taught without pupil devices. Concepts outlast tools.

No pupil accounts

IDEAL Thinkers never needs a pupil to use an AI tool or account. Age limits differ by tool and change often: some tools are for 13 and over with a parent's permission, some for 18 and over. Where a demonstration helps, the teacher runs it on a school approved tool, previews it first and stays in control. Every demonstration lesson can also be taught without one, using prepared results or an unplugged version.

Check your school's position

Follow your school's AI policy and data protection guidance before using any tool, and never enter pupils' personal information. A school approved tool is one your school has checked for data protection and for filtering and monitoring. Check whether pupils' school accounts (for example Google Workspace for Education or Microsoft 365) already give them access to AI tools.

Safeguarding

Four lessons deal directly with privacy, chatbot relationships and images of real people, and five more can prompt disclosures. Each plan's Safeguarding row says what to do, and any disclosure should be handled through your school's safeguarding procedures. Brief your DSL on all nine.

The safeguarding lessons

  • Year 2, Lesson 4: Keep it to yourself
  • Year 3, Lesson 2: Is it always listening?
  • Year 4, Lesson 2: Is a chatbot a friend?
  • Year 6, Lesson 1: Real or generated?

Lessons that can prompt disclosures

  • Year 1, Lesson 6: Boss of the Bot
  • Year 3, Lesson 1: What's in your feed?
  • Year 4, Lesson 6: Chatty's Rulebook
  • Year 5, Lesson 3: Whose is it anyway?
  • Year 6, Lesson 6: AI for Good Design Studio

The teacher demonstration lessons

  • Year 1, Lesson 5: Picture this
  • Year 3, Lesson 3: AI that helps everyone
  • Year 5, Lesson 5: Climb the STAIR
  • Year 5, Lesson 6: Train a class model

There is a page for parents and carers, with letters for each year group, to help schools explain the lessons to families.

The guidance it follows

This approach follows Keeping children safe in education 2026, which lists harmful online interaction with other users or with generative AI applications that simulate this as a contact risk; the DfE's Generative AI in education guidance and Generative AI: product safety standards; and the UKCIS advice on sharing nudes and semi-nudes, which covers AI-generated images.
