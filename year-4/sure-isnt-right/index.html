Curriculum map / Year 4: Machines That Talk

Year 4, Lesson 3

Sure isn't right

Chatbots can sound certain and still be wrong.

IDEAL: AccurateALinks: DL, science

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 3 of 6 in Year 4: Machines That Talk
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain why a chatbot can sound sure and still be wrong.
  • I can decide whether to Trust it, Test it or Bin it.
  • I can use a test, a trusted source or a trusted adult to check an answer.
You need
Printed team card sets (slides 19 to 21), four evidence stations: magnets, spoons, books, calendar
Preparation
About 25 to 30 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge. Plan ahead: Leave the spoons in the room for at least an hour.
What to prepare

Print slides 19, 20 and 21, one set per team of four, and cut out the cards. Slide 20 holds Chatty's eight pre-written answers, so there is nothing else to set up for Chatty. Set up four evidence stations: magnets (a magnet, a steel paperclip, a plastic ruler); spoons (a metal spoon and a wooden spoon, left in the room for at least an hour); book corner (an atlas or UK map, and books or picture cards on minibeasts, the Moon and the human skeleton); calendar.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
None
Prior learning
Builds on earlier lessons in this unit and learning from earlier years. Before teaching this lesson

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • A chatbot's sentence can sound right and still be wrong, because it predicts words.
  • AI learns, spots and guesses, and checking is the person's job.

Routines it uses: Chatty, IDEAL moments, the AI Loop, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • This unit Year 4 Lesson 1: What comes next? (slides 11 to 13)
  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 3: The Mystery Rule (slide 8)
  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 1: Check it! (slides 7 to 8)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 3, Year 2 Lesson 1, Year 4 Lesson 1, Year 4 Lesson 2. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 1 and 3 and 4 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve with RW Q1, Q3 and Q4; if new, use Year 4 Lesson 1 slides 11 to 12 and Year 1 Lesson 3 slide 8 (about 5 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn that confident answers can be wrong, and to check with a test, trusted source or trusted adult.

The signature activity

Chatty's Quiz Show: teams decide Trust it, Test it or Bin it for confident claims, then test them with magnets, spoons, atlases and books.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. True or false? If a chatbot sounds really sure, it must be right. 2. A chatbot says, very sure, "An octopus has six arms." Trust it, Test it or Bin it? How would you check? 3. Name two ways you could check an answer from a chatbot.

Look for pupils who change a decision because of evidence, and who say how they know rather than just right or wrong.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Staying in charge, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
