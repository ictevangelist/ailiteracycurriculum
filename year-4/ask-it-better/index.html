Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain what a prompt is.
  • I can turn a vague prompt into a specific one.
  • I can ask for help for a reason, not as a shortcut.
You need
Printed prompt cards, recipe strips and chef sheets (slides 16 to 18), whiteboards, a timer
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slides 16, 17 and 18. Cut out the eight vague prompt cards (slide 16, one card per pair per round) and the Prompt Chef recipe strips (slide 17, one strip of three per pair). One Prompt Chef sheet (slide 18) per pair. Scrap paper or mini whiteboards and pencils, and a one minute timer. Write a word bank on the board: colours, sizes (tiny, huge), shapes (round, spiky), numbers, body parts and doing words (flying, sleeping).

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • A chatbot predicts its answer from the words so far, so the prompt steers it.
  • People choose the words for AI, and fuller descriptions get closer to what we meant.
  • A hint makes you think; an answer does the thinking for you.

Routines it uses: Chatty, IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • This unit Year 4 Lesson 1: What comes next? (slides 8 and 13)
  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 5: Picture this (slides 8 and 10)
  • Earlier year Year 3 Lesson 4: Hint, not answer (slides 6, 7 and 10)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 5, Year 3 Lesson 4, Year 4 Lesson 1, Year 4 Lesson 3. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 3 and 4 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve with RW Q3 and Q4; if new, use Year 1 Lesson 5 slide 10 and Year 3 Lesson 4 slide 6 (about 4 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn to write specific prompts and to ask AI for help that keeps them learning.

The signature activity

Prompt Chef: pupils give a partner instructions to draw a creature, discover what vague instructions produce, and rewrite them to be specific.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What is a prompt? 2. Make this prompt specific: "Draw a house." 3. Which prompt is better for learning: "Do my homework" or "Give me a hint for question 3"? Why?

Look for pupils who add details that actually matter (number, colour, size, action) rather than just more words, and who can explain why a reason prompt is better for learning than a shortcut prompt.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Learning Well, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
