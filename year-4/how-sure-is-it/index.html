Year 4, Lesson 5

How sure is it?

AI works out what is most likely from patterns, and most likely is not the same as certain.

IDEAL: Informed, AccurateIALinks: CS, maths

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 5 of 6 in Year 4: Machines That Talk
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain that AI works out what is most likely, not what is certain.
  • I can use a confidence score to say how sure I am.
  • I can explain why 90% sure can still be wrong.
You need
Paper circles, pencils and paperclips for spinners, whiteboards, printed sheets (slides 19 to 21)
Preparation
About 15 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge. Plan ahead: Practise the paperclip spinner once beforehand.
What to prepare

Cut one paper circle per pair by drawing round a side plate or saucer on A4 paper. Yellow and grey colouring pencils, a pencil and a paperclip per pair for the spinner. Mini whiteboards and pens. Print slide 19 (one score sheet per team of four), slide 20 (one set of question cards per pair, cut out) and slide 21 (one Honest Guesser sheet per pair). Practise the paperclip spinner once: hold a pencil upright through the end loop of a paperclip at the centre of the circle and flick the paperclip.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
None

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • In the Word Chain Machine, likelier words got more dice numbers but did not always win.
  • A chatbot can sound sure and still be wrong, so we check.
  • AI learns from data to make guesses, not certain answers.

Routines it uses: Chatty, IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • This unit Year 4 Lesson 1: What comes next? (slides 10, 11 and 19)
  • This unit Year 4 Lesson 3: Sure isn't right (slides 6, 9, 10 and 14)
  • Earlier year Year 3 Lesson 1: What's in your feed? (slide 8)
  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 1: Does it guess? (slide 6)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 3 Lesson 1, Year 4 Lesson 1, Year 4 Lesson 3, Year 4 Lesson 4. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 3 and 4 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve with RW Q3 and Q4; if new, use Year 4 Lesson 1 slides 10 and 19 and Year 4 Lesson 3 slide 14 (about 6 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn that AI works out what is most likely, so even a 90% sure answer can be wrong.

The signature activity

Chance Machine: spinners and weather forecasts lead to a class guessing game with confidence scores, showing why 90% sure can still be wrong.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What does "90% chance of rain" mean? 2. True or false? If an AI is 90% sure, it must be right. 3. A bird app says "80% sure: robin." Should you believe it? What would you do?

Look for pupils who lower their confidence when several answers fit the clues, and who explain that a 90% answer can be wrong without the AI being broken.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Staying in charge, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
