Year 4, Lesson 2

Is a chatbot a friend?

Chatbots can sound friendly but do not know or care about you.

IDEAL: EthicalELinks: DC, RSHE

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 2 of 6 in Year 4: Machines That Talk
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain why a chatbot is not the same as a friend.
  • I can compare a chatbot's reply with a friend's reply.
  • I can name trusted adults I can talk to.
You need
Printed cards and record sheets (slides 16 to 18), A4 paper, named school adults list
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge. Plan ahead: Tell your DSL about the lesson beforehand.
What to prepare

Read the safeguarding row above and tell your DSL about the lesson. Print slides 16, 17 and 18: one Two Replies card and one record sheet per pair, and one copy of slide 17 per table. Cut out the four cards on slide 16. Plain A4 paper and coloured pencils for the Helping Hand. Make a list of the adults in school pupils can go to, by name, to share on slide 5 and slide 13.

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • A chatbot picks likely words from patterns and does not know what it is saying.
  • If a machine asks for something private, stop, keep it and tell a grown up you trust.
  • Being honest means telling the truth and not pretending.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • This unit Year 4 Lesson 1: What comes next? (slides 8 and 13)
  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 4: Keep it to yourself (slides 7 and 11)
  • Earlier year Year 3 Lesson 5: Who made it? (slide 5)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 6, Year 2 Lesson 4, Year 3 Lesson 2, Year 4 Lesson 1. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 1 and 3 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve with RW Q1; if new, show Year 4 Lesson 1 slide 13 and Year 2 Lesson 4 slide 11 (about 3 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn why a chatbot is not a friend and who to turn to instead.

The signature activity

Two Replies: compare a chatbot reply and a friend's reply to the same message, then make a Helping Hand of trusted people.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. Give one reason why a chatbot is not the same as a friend. 2. A chatbot says "I missed you!" Is that true? Why does it say it? 3. Name two trusted adults you could talk to if something online worried you.

Look for pupils who use the clue questions to explain their decision rather than how kind a reply sounds, and who can name at least one trusted adult in school.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Staying in charge; Safe, fair and honest, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
