Unbalanced data can lead to unfair results.

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 4 of 6 in Year 5: Data, Training and Fairness
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain how unbalanced training data can make AI unfair.
  • I can choose training data and test who the AI fails.
  • I can explain what balanced data is and why it matters.
You need
Printed Voice Sample and Visitor cards, training and test sheets (slides 15 to 18), envelopes
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slide 15 (Voice Sample cards) and slide 16 (Visitor cards), one set per team of four. Cut out the Visitor cards and put each set face down in an envelope. Print slide 17 (training sheet) and slide 18 (test sheet), one of each per team. Read the "Handling this with care" row above, and adjust Visitor cards 7 and 8 if your school is in Glasgow or Cardiff.

Pupil devices
None needed

Prior learning
Builds on earlier lessons in this unit. Before teaching this lesson

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • A model learns only from its training data, and testing shows what and who it gets wrong.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments.

Where this was taught

  • This unit Year 5 Lesson 2: Train the sorter (slides 5, 6 and 10)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 3 Lesson 2, Year 3 Lesson 3, Year 5 Lesson 2, Year 5 Lesson 3. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When question 2 (slide 2) tests this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve or introduce: show Year 5 Lesson 2 slide 10 and say a model only knows what its training data showed it (3 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils see that unbalanced training data can fail the same people every time, even with a good total score.

The signature activity

With a budget of twelve voice samples, teams choose who to train a school Welcome Bot on, then test it on visitors to see who it fails.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What is bias in AI? 2. What does balanced data mean? 3. A Bot welcomes 11 out of 12 visitors. What should you ask next?

Look for pupils who move beyond the total score to ask who was failed, who explain bias by how the system was trained, tested or designed rather than by anyone's voice, and who suggest testing with every group before a system is used.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Safe, fair and honest; How AI works, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
