Year 5: Data, Training and Fairness

Big question: Where does AI learn from, is it fair, and how do we learn well with it?

By the end of this unit, pupils can tell rules written by people from patterns learned from examples, explain training and test data, ask whose data was used and who might be left out, and plan how AI could help them learn without doing the learning for them.

Before this, in Year 4, pupils studied Machines That Talk. Next, in Year 6, pupils move on to AI, Society and the Future.

How this unit builds

Starting point. AI finds patterns in examples and guesses, so it can be wrong (Y1L2, Y1L3). Missing examples make AI fail some things (Y2L2). Hint, not answer, and try it first (Y2L3, Y3L4). Chatbots predict words, can hallucinate, and work in likelihoods (Y4L1, Y4L3, Y4L5). Vague versus specific prompts (Y4L4).

  1. Lesson 1: Rules or examples?
    Rules versus learning from examples.
  2. Lesson 2: Train the sorter
    Training and test data, and accuracy.
  3. Lesson 3: Whose is it anyway?
    Where training data comes from, and consent.
  4. Lesson 4: The Welcome Bot
    Unbalanced data causes bias.
  5. Lesson 5: Climb the STAIR
    STAIR and Plan, use, prove for learning.
  6. Lesson 6: Train a class model IDEAL Challenge
    Plan, train, test and improve a real model.

Plan ahead. Lesson 1: Build and test the Scratch program yourself first. Lesson 5: Preview both prompts on your school approved AI tool. Lesson 6: Collect clean recycling over a few days; try Teachable Machine once.

Assessment. In Lesson 6 the class plans a balanced, private dataset, trains a real model, tests it on a mystery bag, improves the data and writes an IDEAL model report. How assessment works
