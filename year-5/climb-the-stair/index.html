Climb the STAIR

Using AI to help you learn, not to learn for you.

IDEAL: Learning, DeliberateLDLinks: DL

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 5 of 6 in Year 5: Data, Training and Fairness
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can plan how AI could help me with a learning goal.
  • I can write a STAIR prompt that asks for questions or feedback, not answers.
  • I can prove my learning by explaining it without the tool.
You need
Printed STAIR planners (slide 17), whiteboards, a school approved AI tool or the prepared replies
Preparation
About 15 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge. Plan ahead: Preview both prompts on your school approved AI tool.
What to prepare

Print slide 17 (STAIR planner), one per pair. Preview your school approved AI tool by running the planets prompt (slide 8) and the feedback prompt with Sam's draft (slide 10), so you know what it does. Have whiteboards ready. If you will not use a live tool, read the prepared replies in this plan so you can deliver them confidently as the AI's voice.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
Optional: the teacher runs it on a school approved tool, or uses the prepared results in the plan
Prior learning
Builds on learning from earlier years. Before teaching this lesson

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • A specific prompt gets a more useful reply than a vague one.
  • A hint keeps you thinking; an answer does the thinking for you.
  • AI can sound sure and still be wrong, so facts are checked first.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, Learning Well, STAIR.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 4 Lesson 4: Ask it better (slides 6 and 9)
  • Earlier year Year 3 Lesson 4: Hint, not answer (slides 5 to 7)
  • Earlier year Year 4 Lesson 3: Sure isn't right (slides 8 to 10)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 3 Lesson 4, Year 4 Lesson 3, Year 4 Lesson 4, Year 5 Lesson 4. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 2 and 3 and 4 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Introduce or retrieve: show Year 4 Lesson 4 slide 9, the Prompt Chef recipe (3 minutes). Retrieve: show Year 3 Lesson 4 slide 6, answer or hint (2 minutes). Retrieve: show Year 4 Lesson 3 slide 10, Trust it, Test it or Bin it (2 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils plan prompts that ask AI for questions or feedback, then prove the learning is theirs without the tool.

The signature activity

Pupils plan a learning goal, write STAIR prompts that ask AI to quiz them or give feedback rather than answers, see them run in a teacher demonstration, then explain what they learned without the tool.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. Which STAIR step is missing from this prompt: "Quiz me on the planets." What would you add? 2. In Plan, use, prove, what does the prove step mean? 3. Write one line you could add to a prompt so AI helps you learn, not does it for you.

Look for pupils whose prompts ask the AI to quiz them or give feedback rather than produce answers, who check facts in feedback before acting on them, and who can answer a new question about their goal without their planner.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Learning Well; Staying in charge, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
