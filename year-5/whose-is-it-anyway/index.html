Whose is it anyway?

Training data comes from people's work and lives, so permission matters.

  • I can explain why permission and consent matter.

Print slide 15 (the Robot Art Company letter), fold it and put it in an envelope addressed to "Year 5". Print slide 16 (Permission Charter sheet), one per team of four, plus one enlarged copy for the class's final five rules. Have plain paper and pencils ready for the two minute drawing.

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • A model learns from training data, a large set of examples that people made.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • This unit Year 5 Lesson 2: Train the sorter (slide 5)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 2 Lesson 4, Year 3 Lesson 5, Year 5 Lesson 2. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When questions 1 and 2 (slide 2) test this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Introduce or retrieve: show Year 5 Lesson 2 slide 5 and say AI learns from lots of examples people made (2 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn that training data comes from people's work and lives, so permission and real consent matter.

The signature activity

The Robot Art Company collects pupils' drawings without asking; the class unpicks how that felt and writes a Permission Charter.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. An app wants to train its AI on every picture on the school website. What should it do first, and why? 2. What makes consent a real yes? 3. Write one rule from our Permission Charter, and say why it is fair.

Look for pupils who separate being able to see something from having permission to copy it, and who can explain why "unless you say no" is weaker than asking first.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Safe, fair and honest; How AI works, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
