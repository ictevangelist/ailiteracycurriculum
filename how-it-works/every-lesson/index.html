How it works / Every lesson

Every lesson has the same shape

A consistent structure is designed to lower cognitive load for pupils and teachers alike. The phases are for teachers to plan with; pupils do not need to learn them.

  1. Remember When5 to 7 minFour quick retrieval questions from last lesson, earlier this year and a previous year. Spaced retrieval strengthens long term memory. If the answers show a gap, reteach it briefly before moving on.
  2. Link It5 minA hook that connects to what children already know from their own lives.
  3. Learn It10 to 12 minExplicit teaching of one big idea and its vocabulary, with a worked example and a teacher think aloud. The AI Loop and IDEAL words are always paired with the same pictures (dual coding).
  4. Try It15 to 20 minThe signature activity: collaborative, practical and memorable, usually unplugged.
  5. Own It10 minPupils apply the idea independently or in pairs, usually by creating something.
  6. IDEAL Check3 minWhich IDEAL letter did we practise, and what does it look like in real life?
  7. Exit Ticket3 to 5 minThree short questions to check understanding and shape the next lesson.

Individual lessons adjust these phase timings to suit their activities, and some add a short real-life story. Every plan fits within 60 minutes, most with a few minutes to spare.

45 minute lessons

Shorten the edges and keep the middle: two Remember When questions, a 2 minute Link It, any real life story in one sentence, a 2 minute IDEAL Check and the full exit ticket in 3 minutes. Keep the worked example, the key moment of Try It and Own It; if it still does not fit, use fewer examples, rounds or cards. Each plan says what to cut.

IDEAL moments

In each lesson, the day's IDEAL habit appears as a cue only at the moment pupils practise it. In Years 1 and 2, the teacher points to the cue and the class says the habit together. From Year 3, pupils name the habit the moment needs before the cue appears. Then the teacher asks the action question given in the lesson plan. More on IDEAL

How it's built

Grounded in cognitive science

Spaced retrieval, concrete before abstract, worked examples and modelling, explicit vocabulary teaching, dual coding through consistent visual models, and frequent low stakes checks for understanding.

Designed with UDL

Every plan offers multiple ways to engage, to access content and to show understanding.

Slides built for low cognitive load

Every slide builds on click in the order the teacher teaches it, as if writing on a whiteboard, so pupils meet one element at a time. Visuals carry meaning rather than decoration: each AI Loop step, each IDEAL habit and each recurring idea has its own icon, used identically in every lesson.

Teacher notes, answers and sources are in the lesson plan, not on the screen. New ideas and vocabulary are taught where pupils first need them. Earlier learning is retrieved first, and retaught briefly when Remember When or an exit ticket shows pupils have not got it yet.
