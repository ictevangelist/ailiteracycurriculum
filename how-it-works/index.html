How it works

How the parts fit together

IDEAL Thinkers is the full, teachable AI Literacy strand of the Primary Digital Cognition Curriculum. Each part has one job: what pupils learn, the habits they build, the tools for teaching it, and how a lesson runs.

  1. What pupils learn: five progression strands

    How AI works; staying in charge; safe, fair and honest; learning well; design and create. Each lesson plan names its strands, and the tracker records them. Progression and coverage

  2. The habits they build: IDEAL

    Informed, Deliberate, Ethical, Accurate, Learning, with wording that grows with the children. Learning Well is how the L is taught: Try it first, Hint not answer, then Plan, use, prove. IDEAL Learning Well

  3. The tools for teaching it: the AI Loop and STAIR

    The AI Loop (Learn, Spot, Guess, Check) is a simple model of how AI that learns from examples works. STAIR is a prompting scaffold pupils use in Years 5 and 6. The AI Loop

  4. How a lesson runs: seven phases, for teachers

    Remember When, Link It, Learn It, Try It, Own It, IDEAL Check and Exit Ticket, in 60 minutes. Pupils do not need to learn the phases. Every lesson

  5. What it lines up with

    The Primary Digital Cognition Curriculum, the National Curriculum for computing, SEAME, UDL and cognitive science. Alignment

Every word pupils learn, with its meaning and its picture, is in the glossary.

AI literacy and AI fluency

AI literacy is knowing how the AI children meet works, at a level that fits their age; what it can and cannot do; and how it affects people, fairly or unfairly. Pupils show it by explaining these ideas in their own words and applying them to a new example.

AI fluency is making good decisions about AI: whether to use it at all, what to ask, how to check what comes back, and how to keep the learning their own. In primary school, pupils practise these decisions through unplugged activities and teacher-led demonstrations, not by using AI tools themselves.
