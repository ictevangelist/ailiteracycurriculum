Progression and coverage

How the learning builds

Pupils' learning is tracked in five progression strands, the same five used in the Assessment Tracker, across KS1, Lower KS2 and Upper KS2. The IDEAL habits, Learning Well and the AI Loop are how they are taught. Below: where each AI Literacy outcome is taught, and how it aligns with the National Curriculum.

Alignment

National Curriculum for computing (England), taught directly: KS1, use technology safely and respectfully, keeping personal information private, and know where to go for help (Y1 L6, Y2 L4); recognise common uses of information technology beyond school (Y1 L1, Y2 L5). KS2, use technology safely, respectfully and responsibly, and identify ways to report concerns about content and contact (Y4 L2, Y6 L1); be discerning in evaluating digital content (Y4 L3, Y6 L1); use selection in programs (Y5 L1, Scratch version).

Relevant connections: how feeds choose and rank what we see (Y1 L4, Y3 L1) connects to the KS2 statement on search results, but does not replace teaching search. The current curriculum does not require AI to be taught.

SEAME, from the Raspberry Pi Computing Education Research Centre, is used as a lens to check coverage. For example: engines, Y4 L1 and Y5 L2; models, Y5 L1 and Y5 L6; applications, Y2 L5 and Y3 L1 to L3; social and ethical, Y4 L2, Y5 L3 and Y6 L1 to L5.

The new computing curriculum: BCS is drafting a revised computing curriculum for first teaching from September 2028, and the Curriculum and Assessment Review recommended that it include AI. It is not yet statutory. IDEAL Thinkers will be mapped against the draft when it is published (checked October 2026).

Wider curriculum: RSHE, science, maths, English, geography and careers links are flagged in each lesson plan.

Moving on to secondary school

The Year 7 transition handover sets out, for secondary schools, what pupils can do in each strand by the end of Year 6, the models and words they arrive with, and how KS3 teaching can build on them.
