AI that helps everyone

AI can open doors for people who see, hear or speak differently.

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 3 of 6 in Year 3: AI in Everyday Life
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain what accessible means and why it matters.
  • I can describe how captions, read aloud, translation and describing apps help people.
  • I can test a translation and spot where the AI got it wrong.
Preparation
About 20 minutes, including previewing the demonstration, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Preview the optional demonstration tools (see the teacher demonstration row above). Print slide 16 (station cards) and slide 17 (sentence cards), one set, and cut them out. Set up three stations: Caption it (the three Caption it cards, paper and pencils); Say it right (the three Say it right cards); Describe it (two chairs back to back, two pictures such as postcards or picture book pages with no photos of pupils, paper and pencils). Have a bell or signal to move groups on every four minutes.

Pupil devices
None needed
Demonstration
Optional: the teacher runs it on a school approved tool, or uses the prepared results in the plan

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • When examples are missing, AI gets things wrong for some people, which is unfair.
  • AI tools guess, so their results can be wrong and a person checks.

Routines it uses: IDEAL moments, the AI Loop, Learning Well.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 2: Missing examples (slides 11 to 13)
  • Earlier year Year 1 Lesson 3: The Mystery Rule (slide 8)
  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 1: Check it! (slide 7)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 5, Year 2 Lesson 2, Year 3 Lesson 1, Year 3 Lesson 2. If your class has not had these, ask about pupils' own experience instead.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Retrieve with RW Q3; if new, use Year 2 Lesson 2 slides 11 to 12 and Year 2 Lesson 1 slide 7 (about 4 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn how guessing AI tools can remove barriers, and how to test a translation before trusting it.

The signature activity

Access stations: pupils meet everyday barriers, then see how captions, read aloud, translation and describing apps remove them, including a round trip translation test.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What does accessible mean? 2. Name two ways AI can help someone join in. 3. A translation comes back the same in a round trip. Is it definitely right? Why?

Look for pupils who explain that each tool is guessing, and who think about who might still be left out when the tool makes a mistake.

This evidence feeds the tracker for How AI works; Safe, fair and honest, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
