Hint, not answer

The best help makes you think.

IDEAL: Learning, DeliberateLDLinks: DL

Preparing to teach

Lesson
Lesson 4 of 6 in Year 3: AI in Everyday Life
Time
60 minutes, with a 45 minute version in the plan
Pupils learn
  • I can explain the difference between a hint and an answer.
  • I can sort help from "thinks for me" to "makes me think".
  • I can write hints that help a partner think.
You need
Printed Helper, line end and question cards (slides 15 to 17), a class phrase strip
Preparation
About 10 minutes, plus the plan's Teacher subject knowledge.
What to prepare

Print slide 15 (Helper Cards), one set per pair, and cut them out. Print slide 16 (the two ends of the line), one per pair, and cut each in half. Print slide 17 (question cards), one set per class, and cut them out; the answers are in the table below. No devices are needed. Write the class phrase "Can I have a hint, not the answer, please?" on a strip for your working wall.

Before teaching this lesson

Pupils should already understand

  • Having a go first helps you learn: your brain grows when you do the hard thinking.
  • A helper gives a clue or checks after you try; a doer does it for you.

Routines it uses: Learning Well, IDEAL moments.

Where this was taught

  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 3: Try it first (slides 9 to 11 and 13)
  • Earlier year Year 2 Lesson 3: Try it first (slides 5 and 12)

Remember When (slide 2) recalls Year 1 Lesson 6, Year 2 Lesson 3, Year 3 Lesson 1, Year 3 Lesson 3. These are retrieval questions: if your class has not had those lessons, swap them for a question about pupils' own experience. That does not replace any prerequisite above.

If your pupils haven't covered this

Check first: Remember When question 3 (slide 2) tests this. If most pupils answer well, carry on. If not: Retrieve with RW Q3; if new, introduce with Year 2 Lesson 3 slides 10, 11 and 13 (about 4 minutes).

What this lesson adds

Pupils learn to ask for help that makes them think, and to give hints that keep the thinking with others.

The signature activity

Helper Cards: pupils rank different kinds of help from "thinks for me" to "makes me think", then write hint questions for a partner.

The slides

Teacher notes and answers are in the lesson plan, and printable resources are at the end of the deck.

What to look for

Exit ticket: 1. What is the difference between a hint and an answer? 2. Your friend is stuck on 5 √ó 4. Write a hint, not the answer. 3. When is it OK to get the answer?

Look for pupils who explain who does the thinking with each kind of help, and whose hints keep the thinking with their partner rather than giving the answer away.

This evidence feeds the tracker for Learning Well; Staying in charge, and the unit ends with the IDEAL Challenge. Answers are in the plan.
